# Opportunity Scorecard: Comparative Evaluation Across the Three Areas

## Executive Summary
This document provides a quantitative and qualitative evaluation of the three opportunity areas designated for the Mastercard Opportunity Lab:
* **Area A:** Merchant Payment Disputes & Chargeback Operations (Opp-A: Multi-SaaS Compelling Evidence Auto-Packager)
* **Area B:** Open Finance & Commercial Data Access (Opp-B: Cash Flow Verification & Lending Underwriting Bridge)
* **Area C:** AI-Assisted Financial Operations & Decision Support (Opp-C: Agentic FinOps Copilot & Dynamic Cash Simulator)

Each opportunity is scored on a **1 to 5 scale** across seven explicit criteria aligned with the founder's objective as an Integrated MBA student targeting Product Management, Open Finance Strategy, and Data Analytics & Agentic AI roles at Mastercard.

---

## 1. Evaluation Framework & Weighting Rationale

$$\text{Weighted Score} = \sum_{i=1}^{7} (\text{Score}_i \times \text{Weight}_i)$$

| Evaluation Criterion | Weight | Strategic Rationale for Integrated MBA Founder targeting Mastercard |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **1. Customer Pain Severity & Frequency** | **15%** | Measures whether the problem is existential, recurring, and causes immediate financial or operational damage. |
| **2. Strength & Recency of Evidence** | **15%** | Evaluates whether claims are grounded in current, authoritative primary/secondary research rather than anecdotal speculation. |
| **3. Clear Buyer & Willingness to Pay** | **15%** | Validates whether there is an identifiable business decision-maker with budget authority and demonstrated spending behavior. |
| **4. Product Differentiation** | **15%** | Evaluates whether the solution carves out a defensible whitespace against entrenched incumbents and scheme native tools. |
| **5. Independent Prototype Feasibility** | **15%** | Critical filter: Can a small, independent student team build and test a functioning prototype without confidential data or expensive licenses? |
| **6. Potential Business Value** | **10%** | Assesses addressable market size, unit economics sustainability, and long-term commercial scalability. |
| **7. Relevance to Target Mastercard Roles** | **15%** | Directly measures how effectively the project showcases skills for PM, Open Finance, and Data Analytics & Agentic AI roles at Mastercard. |
| **Total Weight** | **100%** | Balanced distribution ensuring equal emphasis on market need, technical feasibility, and founder career alignment. |

## 3. Deep-Dive Scoring Breakdown per Opportunity

### Opportunity Area A: Multi-SaaS Compelling Evidence Auto-Packager (Opp-A)
* **1. Customer Pain: 4.5 / 5 [E]**  
  Merchants lose $4.61–$5.13 per $1 of direct dispute loss [S-05]; non-refundable $15 fee [S-09]; 41% manual processes [S-05]; 60% dispute abandonment [S-15]. Pain is recurring, direct, and debited directly from bank accounts.
* **2. Evidence Strength: 4.8 / 5 [E]**  
  Backed by authoritative scheme documentation (Mastercom API v6 [S-01], First-Party Trust [S-02]) and independent empirical data (LexisNexis 2025/2026 [S-05], MRC [S-06]).
* **3. Clear Buyer & WTP: 4.5 / 5 [E]**  
  Identifiable buyer: E-commerce Founder / Operations Lead. Proven willingness to pay demonstrated by Chargeflow extracting 25% of recovered revenue [S-11] and merchants buying alerts at $29–$40 [S-14].
* **4. Differentiation: 4.0 / 5 [E]**  
  Disrupts Chargeflow’s aggressive 25% contingency take-rate by offering a transparent utility pricing model ($49/mo or $4/dossier). Adheres strictly to Mastercard Mastercom’s 10MB/18-page rules [S-01, S-13].
* **5. Prototype Feasibility: 4.8 / 5 [E]**  
  **Highest feasibility across all three areas.** Relies entirely on public REST APIs (Shopify Admin, Stripe Disputes, ShipStation, Zendesk). Zero scheme licensing or banking credentials required.
* **6. Potential Business Value: 4.2 / 5 [P]**  
  Over 2 million Shopify merchants and 3+ million WooCommerce stores globally. Low cloud infrastructure cost yields 80%+ software gross margins.
* **7. Relevance to Target Mastercard Roles: 4.2 / 5 [E]**  
  Demonstrates mastery of core card network mechanics, Reason Codes (4837 vs 4853), second presentment compliance, and Mastercard First-Party Trust. Directly maps to Product Management roles in **Cyber & Intelligence Solutions** and **Merchant Operations**.
* **Weighted Score: 4.43 / 5.00 (Rank 1 — Winning Recommendation)**

### Opportunity Area B: Open Finance Commercial Underwriting Bridge (Opp-B)
* **1. Customer Pain: 3.8 / 5 [E]**  
  Small businesses face high bank rejection rates (~80%) and underwriting takes 3–10 days [S-18]. However, the pain is episodic (only experienced when applying for loans).
* **2. Strength & Recency of Evidence: 4.0 / 5 [E]**  
  Authoritative regulatory filings (CFPB Section 1033 Final Rule, Oct 2024 [S-16]) and global architecture standards (Sahamati India AA [S-17]). Evidence against commercial viability is equally verified.
* **3. Clear Buyer & WTP: 3.5 / 5 [P]**  
  Buyer is a community bank or alternative lender. Sales cycles are notoriously long (6–12 months) and require heavy vendor risk assessment.
* **4. Differentiation: 3.0 / 5 [E]**  
  Low differentiation. The pipe layer is dominated by Plaid, Finicity, and MX [S-21], while the underwriting parsing layer is crowded by Ocrolus and Validis.
* **5. Prototype Feasibility: 2.2 / 5 [E]**  
  **Severe Disqualification Barrier.** CFPB Section 1033 strictly excludes commercial accounts [S-16]. An independent student founder cannot obtain production keys to Plaid or Finicity without KYB vetting, SOC 2 Type II compliance, and commercial bank contracts [S-21]. Testing is restricted to synthetic sandbox data.
* **6. Potential Business Value: 3.8 / 5 [P]**  
  High theoretical enterprise value, but unreachable without institutional sponsorship.
* **7. Relevance to Target Mastercard Roles: 4.8 / 5 [E]**  
  Direct hit for "Open Finance Strategy & Commercialization," showcasing understanding of Finicity and CFPB 1033.
* **Weighted Score: 3.56 / 5.00 (Rank 3 — Disqualified for Independent Prototyping)**

### Opportunity Area C: Agentic FinOps Copilot & Cash Flow Simulator (Opp-C)
* **1. Customer Pain: 4.6 / 5 [E]**  
  45% to 50% of small businesses struggle with cash flow crises [S-18]; >50% have unpaid invoices averaging $17,500 [S-18]; 47%+ overdue >30 days. Existential threat to business survival.
* **2. Strength & Recency of Evidence: 4.2 / 5 [E]**  
  Corroborated by Intuit QuickBooks large-scale empirical studies (2024–2025) [S-18] and Mastercard corporate strategy on Agentic AI (Mastercard Agent Pay [S-20]).
* **3. Buyer & WTP: 4.0 / 5 [P]**  
  Small business owners and fractional CFOs pay for accounting software ($30–$150/mo), but SMB software churn is high (20%–30% annually).
* **4. Differentiation: 3.8 / 5 [P]**  
  Must avoid generic conversational chatbots. Differentiates by providing a deterministic, forward-looking operational simulation engine ("what-if" cash cliffs) [S-18, S-19].
* **5. Prototype Feasibility: 4.2 / 5 [E]**  
  Very high. Can be built using open-source agentic LLM orchestration + deterministic Python math engine + QuickBooks/Xero sandbox data.
* **6. Potential Business Value: 4.2 / 5 [P]**  
  Massive addressable base of 33 million US small businesses, with strong potential for B2B expansion into fractional CFO agencies.
* **7. Relevance to Target Mastercard Roles: 4.7 / 5 [E]**  
  Directly aligns with "Data Analytics & Agentic AI" and "Product Management", showcasing skills in autonomous AI agents, tool-calling frameworks, and predictive modeling (mirroring Mastercard Test & Learn and Agent Pay [S-19, S-20]).
* **Weighted Score: 4.25 / 5.00 (Rank 2 — Highly Commended Alternative)**

### Why Area A Outscores Area C:
While Area C (Agentic FinOps) is intellectually exciting and aligns with Mastercard’s AI priorities, **Area A (Dispute Management Auto-Packager) is the stronger independent product prototype candidate** for three decisive reasons:
1. **Zero Hallucination Tolerance:** Financial forecasting in Area C requires 100% mathematical accuracy; any LLM hallucination in cash projections destroys user trust immediately. In Area A, the software acts as an **evidence aggregator and deterministic document assembler**, pulling verified facts (carrier scans, tracking URLs, payment IDs) into a scheme-compliant dossier without algorithmic guesswork.
2. **Immediate Transactional Value Metric:** In Area A, the ROI is direct, measurable, and immediate: *"The merchant contested a $120 chargeback, paid $4 for the dossier, and recovered $120."* In Area C, the value of cash flow advice is diffuse and harder to attribute directly to software ROI.
3. **Card Scheme Technical Depth:** Building Area A allows the founder to demonstrate deep, insider knowledge of card network mechanics—Mastercom API v6 endpoints, Reason Code 4837 vs 4853 requirements, Second Presentment formatting limits, and the First-Party Trust program (RC 5002 Reject). This is the exact domain expertise that sets an MBA product management candidate apart at Mastercard.
